The honest read

13.3 hours/week of class time isn't heavy on its own. What makes this semester demanding is four reading-and-discussion-based courses running in parallel — 214, 285, 301, and 217 are all discussion/seminar format, meaning showing up unprepared isn't really an option the way it might be for a lecture-only course. 74 separate reading assignments over roughly 14 active weeks averages out to 5–6 readings due every week, several of them full book chapters, not just articles — SOC 301 and SOC 217 alone are 24 each. Realistically, budget 10–15 hours/week just for reading and reading-response prep across those four courses, on top of the 13.3 hours in class.

Layer in 3 student clubs (even at a modest 2–3h/week each, that's 6–9h/week) and 2 social-media-manager jobs (SMM work is rarely fixed-hours — content calendars, posting, engagement, and client check-ins usually run 8–15h/week per client/brand, so 16–30h/week combined is realistic), and you're looking at a total weekly load in the range of 48–68+ hours once class, reading, clubs, and both jobs are added together. That's before factoring in the actual writing time for essays, the study time for quizzes/midterms, or the group-coordination overhead for the SOC 301, SOC 217, and SOC/ANT 214 group work — all three of those courses have you working with partners, which adds scheduling friction clubs and solo work don't have.

This is workable, but only if the readings get triaged (mandatory vs. optional — most syllabi mark this clearly) and the club/job hours flex around the crunch weeks below rather than staying fixed. If both jobs are close to full 15h/week each on top of everything else, something will have to give during the weeks flagged below.

Suggestions

Block the reading time as fixed calendar slots the same way class time is fixed — with four discussion-based courses, "I'll read it later" compounds fast. Ask each club whether attendance is flexible around exam weeks; most are. For the two SMM jobs, batch content creation early in the week (e.g., Sunday/Monday) so posting and light engagement — the lower-effort parts — can absorb the high-academic-load weeks flagged above. And treat Nov 9–13 as a protected week: nothing club- or job-related that can move should be scheduled then.
